The path so far

Learning by building, testing, and following the weird thread.

Summer break, 2011

A few hundred MB of data and a heavy laptop

As an adolescent, I shared a tiny phone data connection with a big laptop that had 2 GB of RAM and tried to learn how to code. It ended with my first "Hello World!" in C.

A brochure

An unexpected invitation and a teenage ego boost

A year later, after tinkering on my own, I received an unexpected brochure from a development university. It was flattering, especially that early. To be fair, I had lied about my age while taking their online logic tests.

High school

Wait, I can make things move and light up with code?

Due to family circumstances, I had to leave the pure development branch and go toward electronic engineering. "Son, if you do not become a doctor like your sister, then become an engineer. IT studies have no future." Then I discovered Pic Micro Pascal, and code became physical.

Summer 2015

I graduated and... moved to France

My studies continue in electronics ¯\_(ツ)_/¯

Lab work

Artificial neural networks entered the story

I discovered something I loved: the research, the thinking, the question of how it can be optimized. How can an artificial neural network mimic a biological one? How can we reduce resource cost while generating more artificial neurons and synapses?

Graduation

Crash! Covid arrived right on time

Just as I was graduating, Covid hit and the job market collapsed around me. Not exactly the clean cinematic transition into professional life I had ordered.

Studies

Guess what, I had a Plan B

Back to university for what I always wanted: IT studies. This time through IoT, close to my main subject in the electronics master's degree, but from the software side.

First job

I found a job!

Turns out the skills I learned in electronics and IT were useful in the electric vehicle charging world. I worked as an electrician in a lab, a hardware configurer and installer, a software functional tester, a bug reporter for the devs, and a helpline for clients( I should have never given them my phone number).
